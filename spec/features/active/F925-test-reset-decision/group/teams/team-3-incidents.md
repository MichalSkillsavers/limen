# Incident regressions that the test reset must preserve

Seven distinct failures have evidence of a broken candidate and a fixing commit. One older end-to-end test exposed the Git-index race; the targeted regression was then written after diagnosis. The other named tests followed a review reproduction or a test-first failure. Keep the behavior, not necessarily each test's current implementation. A normal, single-job success test does not replace concurrent or fault-injected checks.

## Valuable refusal checks without a proven historical defect

- `test/land-command.test.ts`, **`land refuses running job, empty commits, dirty target, and unconfirmed merge`**: commit `659c05b` introduced land *and* the refusal test together. The test proves a dirty target is left unchanged; no earlier land implementation or regression was identified. A clean-target scenario cannot prove that refusal, but calling this a previously caught bug would be false.
- `test/finish-webhook-helper.test.ts`, **`helper rejects missing, raw, Basic and malformed Bearer auth before transport`**, **`helper rejects missing or unsafe destinations without revealing their contents`**, **`invalid explicit target lists fail before all transport and never fall back to the single target`**, and **`helper fails redirects, non-2xx, transport errors and a bounded stalled request`**: `5b7cf1e` introduced the Bearer-only helper with its tests; later fan-out added rejection cases. These are security constraints, not a demonstrated prior exploit. The helper fixture intercepts `fetch`; no real outside receiver is contacted. A positive webhook ping does not prove fail-closed routing or that credentials stay on the configured target. Team 2's local-sink rejection table is a suitable replacement *if* it covers each condition.

## What six ordinary seam scenarios miss

The gaps above group into four fault modes: Git observation racing a worker's index; seat registry and stale-lock contention; wake delivery across batched messages or two listeners; and a hosted job with a missing agent row but a live recorded process. The seat registry is outside the six named seams, yet losing its entry removes future reaps and notices. A one-slot **group** contention scenario checks cabinet membership, not the seat registry's dead-owner lock. Do not classify the seat regression as redundant merely because group spawn is concurrent. Dirty-target land and invalid webhook inputs need explicit refusal variants even though their existing tests were preventive.

## Points from other teams

- **Team 2:** They expanded the design from six ordinary seam cases to ten. S3 names batched wake delivery and two listeners; S7 has a zero-request webhook rejection table. They accepted the counterexamples: S9 will seed a dead-owner `projects.lock`, release registrants and pruners together, and require all registrations and zero `ENOTEMPTY` child exits; S1 will force stale tracked-file stat data and check that status does not rewrite the worker index, or retain the small targeted regression if that path differs. Their added cutover rule is stronger: a replacement must fail on the fixing commit's parent and pass on main **before** the old test is deleted. These are designs, not executed checks; I agree they cover the risks if replay proves them.
- **Team 1:** They score `test/sweep-command.test.ts` as a scenario and retain the dead-lock contention shape. They score `test/open-command.test.ts` for deletion despite its discovery of the `index.lock` race; they keep the narrower `test/git-status.test.ts` regression. I agree: discovery history does not require preserving a larger fake-Herdr test when a narrow test or pre-fix-red S1 scenario guards the same behavior. I asked that shorter seat contention tests first fail on the known defective commits: a few green rounds are not proof against an intermittent race.
